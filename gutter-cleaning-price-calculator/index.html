How to price a gutter cleaning job

Measure the gutter runs and group them by height: one story, two stories, three or more. Price each group per linear foot, since a second-story foot takes longer and needs a taller ladder. Add a surcharge when the gutters are packed or overgrown, a price per downspout you flush, and use your minimum charge when the job is small. Small jobs still cost you the drive, the ladder setup and the cleanup.

Then check the price against time: feet cleaned per worker-hour at each height, the condition surcharge, downspout time and setup. Multiply by what a worker-hour really costs you, add supplies and mileage, and compare the margin with your target. The gutter cleaning pricing guide walks through this same example step by step.

How it's calculated

  • Line items = (feet at each height × that height's price per foot) × (1 + debris surcharge) + downspouts × price per downspout.
  • Quoted price = the greater of the line items and the minimum charge (0 if there are no line items), less the recurring discount.
  • Worker-hours use example production rates: 80 ft per worker-hour on one story, 50 on two, 35 on three or more, increased by the debris surcharge, plus 5 minutes per downspout and setup. Hours on site = worker-hours / crew size.
  • Labor = worker-hours × labor cost per worker-hour. Travel = miles × cost per mile.
  • Job cost = labor + supplies + travel. Profit = quote − job cost. Margin = profit / quote. Minimum price = job cost / (1 − target margin).

This is a planning aid, not pricing, safety, tax, legal or accounting advice. Use your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I price a gutter cleaning job?

Measure the gutters in linear feet and split them by roof height, because second- and third-story runs take longer and carry more ladder risk. Price each height per foot, add a surcharge for heavy or packed debris and a line for downspouts, then apply your minimum charge on small jobs. Check the price against labor, supplies and travel before you send it.

What should I charge per linear foot?

There is no single market rate. This calculator ships with example rates ($1.10 per foot on one story, $1.50 on two, $2.00 on three or more, a 25% heavy-debris surcharge, $10 per downspout and a $125 minimum), matching the worked example in the Quoteloom gutter cleaning guide where those exist. Replace them with your own.

Why is labor $28.57 per hour in the example?

It is an example $20 wage divided by 0.70, a rough way to include payroll costs, as in the gutter cleaning guide. Use what one worker-hour really costs your business.

Does this include equipment wear, guards and overhead?

No. The free calculator includes labor, supplies and travel only. The Quoteloom spreadsheet adds gutter-guard installs, equipment wear, monthly overhead, recurring contracts, a printable quote, a job log and a 12-month projection.

Is this pricing or safety advice?

No. It is a planning aid that uses only the numbers you enter, plus the example rates shown on the page. It is not a market survey, safety guidance, insurance quote or tax, legal or accounting advice.
