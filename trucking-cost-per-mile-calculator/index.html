How it's calculated

  • Annual fixed costs = 12 × (truck payment + ELD + other monthly fixed costs) + insurance + permits.
  • Fuel cost per mile = fuel price / MPG (0 if MPG is 0). Variable cost per mile = fuel + maintenance + tires + average tolls.
  • Fixed cost per mile = annual fixed costs / annual miles (n/a if annual miles is 0).
  • Total cost per mile = fixed cost per mile + variable cost per mile + driver pay per mile.
  • Break-even rate per total mile = total cost per mile. Break-even rate per loaded mile = total cost per mile / loaded-mile share (or “Not reachable” if that share is 0).
  • Trip miles = loaded miles + deadhead miles. Load revenue = loaded miles × the per-mile rate.
  • Load cost = total cost per mile × trip miles. Load profit = revenue − load cost.
  • Break-even rate for this load = load cost / loaded miles (or “Not reachable” if loaded miles is 0).

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate trucking cost per mile?

Add up annual fixed costs (truck payment, insurance, permits, ELD and other monthly costs) and divide by your annual miles, including empty miles. Add variable costs per mile: fuel price divided by MPG, plus maintenance, tires and average tolls, plus any per-mile driver pay. The sum is your cost per mile. It is an estimate from the numbers you enter, not a market average.

What is a break-even rate per loaded mile?

You are usually paid only on loaded miles, but you still burn fuel and time on deadhead. The break-even loaded rate is your cost per mile divided by the share of miles that are loaded. If 16% of your miles are empty, you need a higher rate on the paid miles to cover that empty running.

Should I include my own pay as a cost?

A solo owner-operator can leave driver pay at $0 and treat the calculator’s profit as the pay left after running the truck. If you hire a driver, or you want a target wage counted as a cost, enter a per-mile amount. The spreadsheet also supports a percent of revenue or an annual salary.
