How to bid a commercial cleaning contract

Start with the work, not the price. Measure each area, decide how many times a week it is cleaned, and divide square feet by how many square feet one cleaner covers in an hour to get cleaner-hours. Production rates differ for open offices, restrooms and lobbies, which is why the spreadsheet lets you set one per area type.

Then cost the labor honestly: add time for walking, setup and breaks, add paid drive time for every cleaner, and use a loaded hourly cost that includes payroll taxes and insurance, not only the wage. Add supplies, equipment wear and travel, spread overhead across the direct cost, and divide by one minus your target margin. A minimum monthly charge and a rounding step keep a small building from being bid below what it takes to show up.

How it's calculated

  • Cleaner-hours per week = square feet / production rate × cleanings per week. Paid hours = cleaner-hours × (1 + non-productive %) + visits × drive minutes / 60 × crew size.
  • Labor = paid hours × wage × (1 + burden). Supplies = square feet / 1,000 × supplies rate × cleanings per week.
  • Direct cost per week = labor + supplies + equipment wear × visits + travel (visits × miles × cost per mile). Per year = that × weeks serviced.
  • Total annual cost = direct cost + overhead % of direct cost + 12 × fixed monthly contract cost.
  • Monthly bid = total annual cost / (1 − target margin) / 12, never below the minimum monthly charge, rounded up to your step. Annual bid = 12 × monthly. Per visit = annual bid / (visits per week × weeks).

Frequently asked questions

How do I bid a commercial cleaning contract?

Measure the square feet in each area, decide how many times a week each area is cleaned, and turn that into cleaner-hours using your own production rate. Add non-productive time and paid drive time, multiply by your fully loaded labor cost, then add supplies, equipment wear, travel, overhead and any fixed contract costs. Divide the annual cost by one minus your target margin, then by 12 for a monthly bid.

What production rate should I use?

There is no rate on this page you should rely on. The 3,000 sq ft per cleaner-hour shown is an example placeholder so the math is visible. Production rates depend on the building, the equipment and your crew, so time your own work or use a published source you trust, and replace the example.

Does this include overhead and profit?

Yes, as percentages you enter. Overhead is a percentage of direct cost, and your target margin is a percentage of the price. The free calculator also lets you set a minimum monthly charge and round the monthly bid up to a step you choose.
