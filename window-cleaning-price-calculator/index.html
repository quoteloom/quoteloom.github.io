How to price a window cleaning job

Count the windows by floor. Price the outside of each window, then the inside if the customer wants it, then extras: screens, tracks and sills, and hard-water spots, which take far longer than a normal clean and are worth inspecting before you quote. Upper-floor glass needs a ladder or pole and more time, so it carries a multiplier. If the total comes in under your minimum charge, use the minimum: a four-window job still costs you the drive and the setup.

Then check the bid against time. Labor should be what a worker-hour really costs you (wage plus payroll costs), times every worker-hour on the job, including the walk-around and setup. Add supplies and the real round-trip mileage. If the margin is below your target, raise the price to at least the “minimum price for target,” or walk away. The window cleaning bidding guide walks through this same example step by step.

How it's calculated

  • Line items = ground windows × exterior price + upper windows × exterior price × upper-floor multiplier + (if inside too) all windows × interior price + screens, tracks and hard-water windows × their prices.
  • Quoted price = the greater of the line items and the minimum charge (0 if there are no line items), less the recurring discount.
  • Worker-hours use example production times: 5 minutes per ground-floor exterior, 8 per upper-floor exterior, 4 per interior, 2 per screen, 3 per track, 15 per hard-water window, plus setup. Hours on site = worker-hours / crew size.
  • Labor = worker-hours × labor cost per worker-hour. Travel = miles × cost per mile.
  • Job cost = labor + supplies + travel. Profit = quote − job cost. Margin = profit / quote. Minimum price = job cost / (1 − target margin).

Frequently asked questions

How do I price a window cleaning job?

Count the windows (or panes) and price outside and inside separately, then add screens, tracks and any hard-water treatment. Charge more for upper floors that need a ladder or pole, and use your minimum charge on small jobs. Check the bid against labor, supplies and travel, and compare it with the minimum price that hits your target margin.

What should I charge per window?

There is no single market rate. This calculator ships with example prices ($6 per window outside, $5 inside, $3 per screen and per track, $15 per hard-water window, a 1.5x upper-floor multiplier and a $150 minimum), the same example rates as the worked example in the Quoteloom window cleaning guide. Replace them with your own.

Can I price per pane instead of per window?

This free page prices per window. To price per pane, enter pane counts and per-pane prices, but note the example production times assume whole windows. The Quoteloom spreadsheet has separate per-pane prices and production times, plus story multipliers for 3rd floors and above.

Does this include equipment wear and overhead?

No. The free calculator includes labor, supplies and travel only. The spreadsheet also allocates equipment wear and monthly overhead to each job and shows recurring contract value, a printable quote, a job log and a 12-month projection.

Is this pricing or safety advice?

No. It is a planning aid that uses only the numbers you enter, plus the example rates shown on the page. It is not a market survey, safety guidance, insurance quote or tax, legal or accounting advice.
