How to price a pressure washing job

Measure each surface (or use a site estimate you trust) and apply your price per square foot or linear foot. If that comes in below your minimum charge for that service, use the minimum — small jobs still take setup, travel and tank time. Add setup hours, then multiply crew hours by crew size and what each worker actually costs you (wage plus payroll costs, not just the hourly rate you pay out).

Chemicals should follow the surface you are treating, and mileage should include the real round trip, not just the driveway. When the margin on the quote is below your target, raise the price to at least the “minimum price for target,” or decline the job. The example rates on this page are starting points from the Quoteloom workbook (house wash $0.20/sq ft with a $250 minimum, driveway $0.18 with a $150 minimum, deck $0.75 with a $200 minimum, roof soft wash $0.40 with a $400 minimum, gutters $1.50/linear ft with a $125 minimum). They are not market rates.

How it's calculated

  • Quote for each service = the greater of (size × price per unit) and the service minimum. Services at size 0 are skipped. A discount, if any, is applied to the subtotal.
  • Crew hours = size / production rate, plus setup hours when there is any work.
  • Labor = crew hours × crew size × labor cost per worker-hour.
  • Chemicals = size × chemical cost per unit. Travel = miles × cost per mile.
  • Job cost = labor + chemicals + travel. Profit = quote − job cost. Margin = profit / quote. Minimum price = job cost / (1 − target margin).

This is a planning aid, not pricing, tax, legal or accounting advice. Use your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I price a pressure washing job?

Start from the surface size and your price per square foot or linear foot, then apply your minimum charge if the area is small. Add setup time, labor for the whole crew, chemicals and mileage. Compare the quote with the minimum price that hits your target margin before you send it.

What is a typical price per square foot for house washing?

There is no single market rate. This calculator ships with example rates (for instance $0.20 per sq ft and a $250 minimum on a house wash) so the math is visible. Replace those sizes and your own labor rate with what you actually charge and spend.

Does this include equipment wear and overhead?

No. The free calculator includes labor, chemicals and travel only, so you can see those lines clearly. The Quoteloom spreadsheet also allocates equipment wear and monthly overhead to each job and keeps a job log and seasonal projection.

Is this pricing advice?

No. It is a planning aid that uses only the numbers you enter, plus the example rates shown on the page. It is not a market survey, insurance quote or tax, legal or accounting advice.
