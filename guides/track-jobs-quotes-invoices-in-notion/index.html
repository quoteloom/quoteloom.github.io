How to track jobs, quotes and invoices in Notion

Published 2026-11-27 · Quoteloom

A quote in a text thread and an invoice in a different folder is a common way to lose track of what you promised, what you billed, and what is still unpaid. You do not need field-service software to fix that, and a spreadsheet is not the only option.

This guide describes a simple Notion workflow for a one-person or small home-service business: one record for the customer, one for the job, one for the quote, and one for the invoice, linked so you can see the chain. It is a planning approach, not a recommendation to buy any particular template, and not a substitute for bookkeeping software or an accountant. Example figures are labeled as illustrations.

What Notion is good for — and what it is not

Notion is a workspace of pages and databases. You can keep clients, scheduled jobs, quotes and invoices in one place, filter what is unpaid, and write a follow-up from a script. A Free Notion plan, checked November 27, 2026, allows up to 5,000 blocks in a one-person workspace (1,000 if two or more people are in the workspace). That is enough for a lean job tracker if you do not treat Notion as a file dump.

Notion will not collect card payments, send automatic SMS, dispatch a crew on a map, or sync to QuickBooks by itself. If you need those things, this is the wrong tool. The documents you send the customer — a PDF quote and a PDF invoice — can still be made in the free Quote Maker and Invoice Maker, which run in your browser and do not upload your customers to Quoteloom. Notion holds the record; the PDF is what the customer keeps.

The records to keep

  • Clients. Name, phone, email, service address, and whether they are a lead, an active customer, or someone you should not contact.
  • Jobs. The visit: trade, status (scheduled, done, cancelled), date, address, and links to the quote and invoice.
  • Quotes. Quote number, date, status (draft, sent, accepted, declined), line-item total, discount, tax and the total you sent.
  • Invoices. Invoice number, date, amount, amount paid, amount due, and status (draft, sent, paid).
  • Line items, if you want them. Each service on the quote or invoice, with quantity and unit price, so you can see what a “house wash + driveway” actually included six months later.

Optional, but useful: a services-and-rates list you quote from, a checklist for the trade, and a few follow-up scripts you can copy. Send only what is true for that job. Do not invent deadlines or reviews, and do not offer a discount in exchange for a review.

A simple path through a job

  1. Inquiry. Add or update the client. Note how they found you, if you care about that later.
  2. Price the work. Use your rate list and, if you want a cost check, the free pressure washing, house cleaning or lawn care calculator. Write a walk-away number before you talk price; see minimum charges and walk-away prices.
  3. Send a quote. Put the scope, exclusions, line items, total and a validity date on a PDF. How to write a house cleaning or pressure washing quote covers the document. Log the same totals in Notion as a quote row, status Sent.
  4. Book the job. When they accept, mark the quote Accepted, add a job with the date, and (if you take a deposit) record it.
  5. Do the work, then invoice. Invoice the same lines you quoted unless you agreed a change. The Invoice Maker can import a Quote Maker draft in the same browser. Log the invoice in Notion, with amount paid and amount due.
  6. Follow up only if needed. A “job complete” message or a polite unpaid-invoice reminder should match what actually happened. Stop when the invoice is paid; mark it Paid.

Worked example: the $866.40 pressure washing job

Illustration only. This is the same default job as the pressure washing pricing guide and the quote-writing guide: house wash $480.00, driveway $162.00, gutters $270.00, subtotal $912.00, 5% discount $45.60, total $866.40.

  • In Quotes, store quote Q-1001, status Sent, then Accepted, with those line totals and a total of $866.40.
  • In Jobs, store the visit (trade: pressure washing, status: scheduled, then complete) and link it to Q-1001 and to the customer.
  • In Invoices, store INV-1001 for $866.40. If no deposit was paid, amount due is $866.40. If a 20% deposit of $173.28 was already received, amount due is 866.40 − 173.28 = $693.12 until the balance arrives, then $0.00 and status Paid.

The point of the database is not prettier math. It is so you can answer, later: was this quote accepted, was the job done, and has the invoice been paid?
