How to price ceramic coating, including prep and correction

Published 2026-11-27 · Quoteloom

A ceramic coating job is mostly prep. The bottle is only one line. Wash, decontamination, polishing or correction, panel prep, application and cure time usually take far longer than wiping the product on, and a coating locks in whatever is already in the paint. If you quote from a shop’s advertised package without timing those steps, the job is easy to underprice.

This page separates a spray sealant add-on from a multi-year professional coating, then works one coating illustration through the same cost check as the free mobile detailing calculator: paid labor (including the drive), supplies, mileage and a target margin. Labor rate, drive time, mileage rate and the 40% target used below are the calculator’s example defaults. Hours on the vehicle and product cost are labeled illustrations, one of them taken from a published 2026 operator example, not measured times and not a typical job. A spray sealant is not a professional coating

The Quoteloom mobile detailing calculator and the mobile detailing pricing guide include a ceramic spray sealant add-on at an example $80 and 45 worker-minutes. That line is a short protective spray on top of a wash or a detail. It is not a multi-year professional coating, and this page does not treat $80 or 45 minutes as a coating price or a coating time.

A professional coating is a different service: longer prep, a different product, often a warranty that depends on maintenance washes, and usually a full day or more on the calendar. Price it as its own package, after you have seen the paint, or give only a starting range and confirm the correction level after inspection.

Published ranges, and what they are not

A 26 October 2026 operator pricing guide from DetailRoam, written for detailers, lists these as tiers it says are often seen in many parts of the United States for a midsize car, and tells readers to use them as a sanity check, not as a price list:

  • Entry (about a one- to two-year coating with a wash, decontamination and light polish): often $500 to $900
  • Mid (a multi-year coating with a one-step correction): commonly around $900 to $1,800
  • Premium (a longer-lasting coating with multi-stage correction, wheels and glass included): usually $1,800 and up

Those figures are that guide’s stated ranges, not Quoteloom survey data, not a national average, and not a recommended bid. Your market, your paint, and your hours will differ. The same article’s pricing formula is: estimate hours, multiply by the hourly rate you need, add product cost, then add overhead and margin.

Worked example: a midsize SUV coating, checked with calculator costs

DetailRoam’s own illustration, in that same 26 October 2026 article, is a midsize SUV with average paint that “takes ten hours from start to finish,” a target of $85 an hour, and “product and supplies run about $120.” It adds 15% for overhead and margin and “land[s] near $1,115,” then suggests rounding to a package such as $1,099 or $1,149. The ten hours, the $120 of product and the $1,099 package used below are that illustration, reused here so the arithmetic can be checked. They are not typical times, not typical product cost, and not a recommended price.

This page does not use DetailRoam’s $85 billing rate as a cost. It checks the $1,099 package against the free calculator’s example cost defaults: labor at $22.00 per paid hour (a stand-in for wage plus payroll costs, not a wage recommendation), a one-person crew, a 14-mile one-way trip, 22 minutes each way, $0.70 per mile for the vehicle (round trip), and a 40% target margin. The $22.00 rate is a cost, not what you charge the customer. Equipment wear and monthly overhead are left at $0, as on the live calculator. Replace every figure with your own.

  • Worker time on the vehicle: 10.00 hours (the cited illustration, treated here as already including setup)
  • Drive time: 22 × 2 = 44 minutes = 0.733 hours
  • Paid hours: 10.00 + 0.733 = 10.733 hours
  • Labor cost: 10.733 × $22.00 = $236.13
  • Product and supplies: $120.00 (the cited illustration, not the calculator’s $40 chemicals default, which is meant for a regular detail)
  • Vehicle cost: 14 × 2 × $0.70 = $19.60
  • Total cost = 236.13 + 120.00 + 19.60 = $375.73
  • Quoted package: $1,099.00 (no travel fee in this illustration; fold travel into the package or add the calculator’s fee if that is how you bill)
  • Profit = 1,099.00 − 375.73 = $723.27. Margin = 65.81%
  • Minimum price for a 40% margin = 375.73 ÷ 0.60 = $626.22. Status: meets the example target on these inputs. The margin looks wide because $22.00 is a labor cost, not an $85 billing rate, and because equipment wear and overhead are left out, as they are on the free calculator.

If the paint needs more correction and the same job takes 14 hours on the vehicle instead of 10, paid hours become 14.733, labor becomes $324.13, and total cost becomes $463.73. The minimum price for a 40% margin is then 463.73 ÷ 0.60 = $772.88. The $1,099 package still clears that floor on these costs, but the extra four hours are the reason to inspect the paint before you lock the number. These results are not typical margins.

Questions

Is the calculator’s $80 ceramic add-on a professional coating price?

No. That example $80 and 45-minute line is a ceramic spray sealant add-on on a wash or a detail. A multi-year professional coating is a different service with much more prep. Do not use the $80 add-on as a coating bid.

Should I quote a coating before I see the paint?

Give a starting range if you must, then confirm after inspection. Correction time is usually the largest variable. This page does not prescribe a booking policy.

Are $500 to $1,800 typical coating prices?

This page does not say so. Those figures are tiers published in a 26 October 2026 DetailRoam guide as a sanity check for a midsize car, not a survey Quoteloom ran, and not a recommended bid. The $1,099 worked example is that article’s rounded illustration, checked against the calculator’s example costs.

Is this pricing advice?

No. It is a planning layout with a worked example. It is not a market rate, not a forecast, and not tax, legal, insurance or business advice. Verify every number, including product cure requirements and your own insurance, before you send a quote.
