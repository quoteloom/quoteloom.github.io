How to create an owner statement for a vacation rental, with a worked example

Published 2026-11-27 · Quoteloom

An owner statement is a simple monthly report for one property: what came in, what went out, and what was left. Hosts use it for themselves; managers use it when they report to an owner. This page walks through the arithmetic, then works one fictional April month in full so you can check every total.

It is a planning aid, not a tax form, not a bank reconciliation, and not investment, legal or accounting advice. The example people, addresses, rates and fees are labeled EXAMPLE. They are not market rates, occupancy data or a forecast. Replace them with the amounts you actually received and paid.

What belongs on the statement

Keep one statement per property per month (or per the period you actually report). A useful set of lines is:

  • Income (payouts). The money that arrived after a booking site’s channel fee, or the rent that arrived on a long-term unit. This is not the same as the guest’s booking total.
  • Cleaning costs. What you paid a cleaner, a laundry service, or yourself for turnovers in that period — which may differ from the cleaning fee a guest paid.
  • Other expenses. Supplies, utilities, insurance, landscape, and similar costs you assign to that property and month.
  • Maintenance. Repairs completed and paid in the period.
  • Net. Income minus those three cost groups.

The formulas this example uses:

Stay amount = nights × nightly rate (for a stay)

Booking total = stay amount + guest cleaning fee

Channel fee = booking total × channel fee percent

Payout = booking total − channel fee

Statement income = sum of payouts in the period (or the month’s rent on a long-term unit)

Net = income − cleaning costs − other expenses − maintenance

The guest cleaning fee is already inside the booking total, and therefore inside the payout. On the statement you then subtract what the turnover actually cost you. Those two cleaning numbers are often different. The turnover checklist guide works that gap in more detail; this page uses the cost you paid.

Worked example: Oak Lane Cottage, April 2026

All figures below are the same worked example used in Rental Host OS. They are independently recomputed here. They are illustrations, not a market rate and not a forecast.

The example short-term unit, Oak Lane Cottage, had three completed stays in April. Each stay is billed at $185.00 a night with a $125.00 guest cleaning fee. A 3% channel fee is applied to the booking total on every stay (including a direct booking in this example) so that one formula covers the month. 3% is an illustration, not a platform’s published fee.

  • B-1001 (3 nights): stay 3 × 185.00 = $555.00, booking total 555.00 + 125.00 = $680.00, channel fee 680.00 × 0.03 = $20.40, payout 680.00 − 20.40 = $659.60.
  • B-1002 (4 nights): stay $740.00, booking total $865.00, channel fee $25.95, payout $839.05.
  • B-1003 (2 nights): stay $370.00, booking total $495.00, channel fee $14.85, payout $480.15.

April payouts: 659.60 + 839.05 + 480.15 = $1,978.80. That is statement income. The three booking totals are $2,040.00, and the three channel fees are $61.20; 2,040.00 − 61.20 = 1,978.80, which matches.

Three turnovers were paid at $80.00 each, so cleaning costs are $240.00. Other April expenses on this unit are supplies $35.00 and internet $60.00, together $95.00. One paid repair, a bathroom faucet washer, is $18.00.

Net for Oak Lane, April 2026:

1,978.80 − 240.00 − 95.00 − 18.00 = $1,625.80

The same month, the example long-term unit Maple Court Flat collected $1,400.00 of rent, had no cleaning cost and no maintenance in the period, and had expenses of landscape $40.00 plus an insurance share $55.00 (a split chosen for this example, not an official premium), together $95.00. Net is 1,400.00 − 95.00 = $1,305.00.

Combined example net for the two properties is 1,625.80 + 1,305.00 = $2,930.80. Keep a statement per property; add them only if you want a portfolio total. This combined figure is not a return and not a reason to buy or list a unit.

Questions

What is an owner statement?

A short report, usually monthly and usually one property at a time, that adds the money that arrived (payouts or rent) and subtracts cleaning costs, other expenses and maintenance paid in that period. The remainder is the net for that month. It is a record, not a tax return.

Should income be the guest’s booking total or the payout after fees?

Use the payout — what actually arrived after a channel fee — or the rent that actually arrived. If you enter the booking total as income and also subtract the channel fee, you can get the same net, but do not count the booking total as if it were cash you received.

Where does the guest cleaning fee go?

It is already inside the booking total, so it is already inside the payout once the channel fee is taken off. On the statement, subtract what you paid for the turnover, which may be a different amount. Do not add the guest fee a second time as income.

Is this tax, accounting or investment advice?

No. It is a planning layout with a worked example. How you report income and expenses to a tax authority is a separate question for your adviser. This page does not tell you what you can deduct.
