How to track maintenance across rental properties, with a simple log

Published 2026-11-27 · Quoteloom

When you host or let more than one unit, repairs are easy to lose: a photo in a message thread, a receipt in a wallet, a promise to “do it before the next check-in.” A single log, with a property on every row, is enough to see what is open, what you have already paid, and which unit is absorbing the work.

This page is a starting layout and a worked example. It is not a building code, a safety inspection, a warranty, or legal, tax or investment advice. You still need a qualified person for work that requires a license. The people, dates and amounts below are labeled EXAMPLE. They are not typical repair costs and not a forecast.

What to write on each row

One row per issue, not per photo. Include at least:

  • The property — the unit the work belongs to, so you can filter a month or a year by property.
  • The issue — a short, specific name (“dripping bathroom faucet”), not just “fix.”
  • Date reported and date done — or a blank done date while the item is still open.
  • Status — open, scheduled, or done. Only “done” rows should be added into a paid total.
  • Cost — what you actually paid, once you have a receipt or a paid invoice. An estimate can sit in a note; do not add estimates into “spent.”
  • Who did the work and a note or photo reference if you have one.

If you also keep a monthly owner statement, tag the row with that month once the cost is paid, so the statement’s maintenance line is just the sum of those done rows. The faucet repair in the example below is the same $18.00 April item used on the Oak Lane Cottage statement.

Turnovers and ordinary supplies are not maintenance. A missed stain or an empty soap bottle belongs on the turnover checklist. A leaking trap or a failed smoke-alarm battery belongs here.

Worked example: two properties, spring 2026

The log below is fictional. Oak Lane Cottage is an example short-term unit; Maple Court Flat is an example long-term unit. “Done” means the work was finished and paid. “Open” means it has been reported and is not finished; the $22.00 on that row is an estimate only and is not included in the paid totals.

  • Oak Lane, 12 April 2026 — dripping bathroom faucet — done — $18.00.
  • Maple Court, 28 April 2026 — bedroom window latch — done — $40.00.
  • Oak Lane, 3 May 2026 — smoke-alarm batteries — done — $24.00.
  • Maple Court, 15 May 2026 — HVAC filter and service — done — $165.00.
  • Oak Lane, 22 May 2026 — dishwasher not draining — done — $95.00.
  • Maple Court, 2 June 2026 — dripping kitchen faucet — open — estimated $22.00, not paid.

Paid totals (done rows only):

  • Oak Lane Cottage: 18.00 + 24.00 + 95.00 = $137.00 (three items).
  • Maple Court Flat: 40.00 + 165.00 = $205.00 (two items). The open faucet is not in this total.
  • Both properties: 137.00 + 205.00 = $342.00 paid. Five items done, one still open.

Those totals are not a reserve, a budget, or a percentage of property value. They are only the sum of the paid rows in this illustration. If you later pay $22.00 on the open Maple Court faucet, the Maple paid total would become 205.00 + 22.00 = $227.00, and the combined paid total would become $364.00. Until it is paid, leave it out of “spent.”

A log like this also shows whether one unit is getting all of the work, and how long an item sat open. It does not tell you whether a unit is a good investment. Questions

What should I record for each repair?

The property, a specific issue name, the date it was reported, the date it was finished (or a blank while it is open), the status, the amount you actually paid once you know it, and who did the work. Keep estimates in a note until they are paid.

Should an estimate count toward what I have spent?

No. Add a row when the issue is reported so it does not disappear, but only sum the rows whose status is done and whose cost is a paid amount. In the example above, the $22.00 kitchen faucet is left out of the $342.00 paid total.

Do I need property-management software to do this?

No. A spreadsheet or a simple database is enough if every row has a property and a status. A template can save setup time; it is not required to keep the log.

Is this a recommended maintenance reserve?

No. This page does not tell you to set aside a percentage of rent or of property value. It only shows how to total the work you have already paid, by property.
