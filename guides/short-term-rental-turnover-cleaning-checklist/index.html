A short-term rental turnover cleaning checklist, with the cost math

Published 2026-11-27 · Quoteloom

A turnover is the work between one stay and the next: clean the unit, remake the beds, restock what you provide, and catch anything that is broken before the next guest arrives. The cleaning fee a guest pays is not automatically equal to what that work costs you.

This guide is a starting checklist and a worked cost example, so you can see the gap (or the leftover) on the cleaning line. It is not a health, safety or legal standard, not a staffing plan, and not a market survey. Example times, wages and fees are labeled as illustrations. Replace them with the times you actually record and the amounts you actually pay and charge.

The cleaning fee and the cleaning cost are different numbers

Guests often pay a cleaning fee on top of the stay. You often pay a cleaner (or pay yourself) a different amount, and you may also buy supplies and pay for travel or laundry. If a booking site charges a percentage fee on the whole booking, that percentage usually applies to the cleaning fee too. Net cleaning revenue is then:

Net cleaning revenue = guest cleaning fee − (guest cleaning fee × channel fee percent)

Turnover cost = labor you pay + supplies + travel (and laundry, if you pay it separately)

Leftover on the cleaning line = net cleaning revenue − turnover cost

A leftover is not the profit of the stay. Housing costs, utilities and empty nights still have to be covered by the stay itself. Use the short-term rental profit calculator and the profit guide for that fuller picture. This page is only the turnover.

A practical turnover checklist

Tick the steps that apply. Skip the ones that do not. Walk the unit in a consistent order so you can tell, later, whether a missed item is a one-off or a pattern.

Before you enter

  • Confirm the checkout time and that the guests have left.
  • Bring the supply tote and the linens for this unit.
  • Note any damage or missing items on arrival, with a photo.

Rooms

  • Strip beds and start the laundry, or bag linens if a service does it off-site.
  • Remake beds with clean linens.
  • Wipe surfaces, switches and remotes.
  • Vacuum and mop floors.
  • Empty bins and replace bags.

Kitchen and bath

  • Clean kitchen surfaces, the sink, the hob and the inside of the microwave.
  • Run or empty the dishwasher; restock detergent if you provide it.
  • Clean the bathroom, including the toilet, sink, shower and mirror.
  • Restock soap, toilet paper and trash bags to your usual levels.

Before you leave

  • Reset the thermostat to your usual setting.
  • Check that lights, locks and the trash are in the usual state.
  • Take after-clean photos if that is your habit.
  • Report anything that needs a repair, with a photo, so it can be fixed or disclosed before the next arrival.

Do not keep door codes, lockbox codes or Wi-Fi passwords on an unsecured shared page. Send access notes only through the channel you actually use, to the person who needs them.

Worked example: a 3-hour turnover

Illustration only — not a wage survey and not a recommended fee. Suppose a two-bedroom unit, checkout at 11:00 and the next arrival at 16:00. You (or a cleaner) spend 3.0 hours on site. You pay $25.00 per hour for that labor (the amount you actually pay, including any payroll costs you bear, not “a typical rate”). Supplies for this visit are $10.00. The round trip is 12 miles. For a mileage cost, this example uses the IRS business standard mileage rate of 76 cents per mile, which applies July 1 through December 31, 2026 (see Sources). That is a cost figure for planning, not tax advice; use your own records if you deduct mileage.

  • Labor: 3.0 × 25.00 = $75.00
  • Supplies: $10.00
  • Travel: 12 × 0.76 = $9.12
  • Turnover cost: 75.00 + 10.00 + 9.12 = $94.12

If the guest cleaning fee on this stay is $125.00, the leftover on the cleaning line before any channel fee is 125.00 − 94.12 = $30.88.

If a 3% channel fee applies to the cleaning fee as well, the fee on that line is 125.00 × 0.03 = $3.75, net cleaning revenue is $121.25, and the leftover is 121.25 − 94.12 = $27.13.

If you instead paid a flat $80.00 to a cleaner and had the same $10.00 of supplies and $9.12 of travel, turnover cost would be $99.12, and the leftover after a 3% fee on a $125.00 guest fee would be 121.25 − 99.12 = $22.13. A flat cleaner fee is often simpler; it is only a good deal if it actually covers the time and travel on this unit.

Three same-cost turnovers in a month, at $94.12 each, are $282.36 of cleaning cost. That is the kind of total that belongs on a monthly owner statement, next to the stay payouts and other expenses, not as a substitute for them.
