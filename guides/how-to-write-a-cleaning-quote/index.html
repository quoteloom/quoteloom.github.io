How to write a house cleaning or pressure washing quote

Published 2026-11-27 · Quoteloom

A customer who asks for a quote is trying to understand two things: what you will do, and what it will cost. A number in a text message answers only the second question, and it is easy for both of you to remember the job differently later.

This guide is about the document, not the first-time pricing math. You still need a price you can stand behind — the house cleaning and pressure washing pricing guides, and the free calculators, cover that. Here you will put that price on a written quote with a clear scope, line items, exclusions, and a validity date, then check it against cost. Example figures show the layout. They are not a rate card for your city, and nothing here is a promise that a customer will accept.

Price the job before you write the quote

Write the quote after you know the work, the travel, and a floor you will not go below. If you have not priced the visit yet, start with the matching guide and calculator:

Those pages also cite 2026 homeowner-facing ranges from Angi and HomeAdvisor, which are national figures, not your rate. Use your own times and costs.

What to put on every quote

Use the same sections every time so you do not forget a line when you are in a hurry. The free Quote Maker can format these into a PDF in your browser; you still have to type what is true for the job.

  • Who you are. Business name, a phone number or email the customer can actually reach, and your address if you put one on documents.
  • Who the customer is, and where the work is. A billing name is not always the same as the service address. Write both if they differ.
  • Quote number and date. A simple number you can find later (for example Q-20261127-01) is enough.
  • The scope, in lines the customer can recognize. “Standard clean, 3 bedrooms, 2 bathrooms, inside fridge” is clearer than “cleaning.” “House wash, driveway, gutters” is clearer than “pressure washing.”
  • What is not included. If ovens, interiors of cabinets, second-story glass, roofs, or oil-stain treatment are extra, say so on the quote. Silence is how arguments start.
  • The price, by line, then a total. Show each service or add-on, any frequency discount, and the total the customer would pay. If you charge sales tax, show it as its own line on the amount you actually tax. If you do not, do not invent a tax line.
  • A validity date. “This quote is good through 11 December 2026” is a real date. Do not write “only two slots left this week” unless that is true, and do not invent a deadline to pressure someone.
  • How they accept, and how they pay. How to reply, whether a deposit is required to book, and when the balance is due. Write only the terms you actually use.

Worked example A: a pressure washing quote

This is the same default job as the pressure washing pricing guide and the free calculator: a 2,400 sq ft house wash, a 900 sq ft driveway, and 180 linear feet of gutters, with a 5% discount. The figures are an illustration, not a bid for a real house.

  • House wash: 2,400 × $0.20 = $480.00
  • Driveway: 900 × $0.18 = $162.00
  • Gutters: 180 × $1.50 = $270.00
  • Subtotal: $912.00
  • Discount 5%: 912.00 × 0.05 = $45.60
  • Quote total: 912.00 − 45.60 = $866.40

On the document, list those three services as separate lines, show the 5% discount, and write what is not included (for example, no roof, no oil-stain treatment, no sealing). If you ask for a 20% deposit to hold the date, 0.20 × 866.40 = $173.28 due to book, and $693.12 due when the job is finished. Only use a deposit if that is actually your term.

The same guide’s cost check on this job is labor $232.10 + chemicals $62.40 + travel $16.80 = $311.30 of direct cost, so profit at $866.40 is $555.10. That cost check does not have to appear on the customer’s PDF, but you should run it before you send the number. The free calculator, and that worked example, do not allocate equipment wear or monthly overhead; if you ignore those, the margin on paper is fatter than the margin you keep.

Worked example B: a house cleaning quote

This is the default job in the house cleaning pricing guide: an 1,800 sq ft, 3-bed, 2-bath standard clean, priced by the room, biweekly, plus an inside fridge, with a 15% frequency discount. Illustration only.

  • Cleaning price before discount: $60 + 3 × $20 + 2 × $25 = $170.00
  • Biweekly 15% discount: 170.00 × 0.85 = $144.50
  • Inside fridge add-on: $30.00 (not discounted in that example)
  • Quote total: 144.50 + 30.00 = $174.50

Write “standard biweekly clean” and the fridge as separate lines, and name what a standard clean does not include (for example, insides of ovens or windows, if those are add-ons for you). The pricing guide’s cost check on this visit is labor $85.80 + supplies $9.00 + travel $9.10 = $103.90, so profit at $174.50 is $70.60 (about 40.5% of the quote), which meets that example’s 30% target. The minimum price for a 30% margin would be 103.90 / 0.70 = $148.43. The $174.50 quote has room; that does not mean a deeper discount would still work.
