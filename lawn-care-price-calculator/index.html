How to price lawn care and seasonal contracts

Convert the lot to square feet if you think in acres (one acre = 43,560 sq ft). Price each service as the greater of (square feet / 1,000 × your price per 1,000 sq ft) and your minimum for that stop, then multiply by the number of visits. A small yard can still take a full setup, which is why a minimum matters.

Add the services together for the season, then apply a contract discount only if the package really saves you selling time or fuel. Divide by the months you will bill to get a monthly payment. Cost should include crew labor, hours of equipment time, travel on each trip, fertilizer or other materials, and any disposal fees. If the margin is below your target, raise the contract or cut visits you cannot staff profitably.

How it's calculated

  • Visit price for each service = the greater of (lot sq ft / 1,000 × price per 1,000 sq ft) and the service minimum, times the visit count.
  • Season contract = sum of visit prices × (1 − discount). Monthly payment = contract / months billed.
  • Hours = sum of (visits × lot sq ft / production rate) + trips × setup minutes / 60, where trips = the highest visit count.
  • Labor = hours × crew × labor cost per worker-hour. Equipment = hours × equipment cost per hour. Travel = trips × miles × cost per mile.
  • Season cost = labor + equipment + travel + materials + disposal. Minimum contract = season cost / (1 − target margin).

Example rates: mowing $6 / 1,000 sq ft ($40 minimum), edging and trimming $1.50 / 1,000 sq ft ($10 minimum), leaf cleanup $14 / 1,000 sq ft ($120 minimum, $25 disposal), fertilizing $5 / 1,000 sq ft ($45 minimum, $2.20 materials), aeration $9 / 1,000 sq ft ($80 minimum). These are examples, not market rates. This is a planning aid, not pricing advice.

Frequently asked questions

How do I price a lawn care contract?

Price each service visit from the lot size (with a minimum so small yards still cover a stop), multiply by how many visits you will make that season, then subtract a contract discount if you offer one. Divide the season total by the months you bill to get the monthly payment. Check that the contract still clears your target margin after labor, equipment time, travel, materials and disposal.

Should I charge per visit or by the season?

Either can work. A seasonal contract with a monthly payment smooths revenue and is easier to explain as a package. Per-visit pricing is clearer when the schedule changes week to week. This calculator builds a season total and a monthly payment so you can compare both views of the same work.

Does this include monthly overhead?

No. The free calculator includes crew labor, equipment time, travel, materials and disposal fees. The Quoteloom spreadsheet also allocates monthly overhead across your yearly visits and includes a job log and a 12-month projection.

Are the example prices typical rates?

No. Example figures (for instance mowing at $6 per 1,000 sq ft with a $40 minimum) come from the Quoteloom workbook so the formulas are visible. They are not a market survey. Use your own rates, wages and visit counts. This is a planning aid, not pricing, tax, legal or accounting advice.
