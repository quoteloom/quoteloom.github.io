How to price a house cleaning job

Choose the method you already use. Square-foot pricing scales smoothly on large homes. Room pricing (a base plus a charge per bedroom and bathroom) is easier to explain at the door. Hourly pricing follows your production rate: hours = square feet / square feet per hour, then times your hourly rate, with a minimum so a tiny job still covers the visit.

If you discount weekly or biweekly work, apply that to the cleaning price only, then add extras (ovens, fridges, windows, laundry) at their full rate — otherwise a “cheap” recurring clean plus several add-ons will hide a loss. Labor should be what each cleaner costs you per hour, multiplied by paid hours for the whole crew, including drive time. If the margin lands below your target, the quote is too low, the discount is too big, or the visit needs more time than you thought.

How it's calculated

  • Cleaning price = square feet × rate, or base + beds + baths, or estimated hours × hourly rate — then the greater of that figure and the service minimum.
  • Quoted price = cleaning price × (1 − frequency discount) + add-ons, then any extra discount.
  • Time on site = (square feet / production rate + add-on minutes / 60) / crew size.
  • Labor = labor cost per cleaner-hour × (time on site + drive time) × crew size. Travel = miles × cost per mile.
  • Job cost = labor + supplies + travel. Minimum price = job cost / (1 − target margin).

Example add-on prices: oven $35 (40 min), fridge $30 (30 min), window $5 (6 min), laundry load $15 (20 min). This is a planning aid, not pricing advice. Use your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How should I price a house cleaning job?

Pick a method that matches how you already quote: by square foot, by bedrooms and bathrooms, or by the hour. Apply your minimum charge, then subtract a recurring-frequency discount if you offer one, and add extras such as ovens or windows. Labor should include paid drive time for the whole crew, not only time inside the house.

How much should I discount weekly or biweekly cleans?

Only if the repeat work really saves time and selling cost. The example discounts on this page are 20% weekly, 15% biweekly and 10% monthly, taken from the Quoteloom workbook so the math is visible. They are not a rule. If a discounted price falls below your minimum price for the target margin, the discount is too steep.

Does this include overhead and equipment?

No. The free calculator includes labor, supplies and travel only. The spreadsheet also adds a per-job equipment amount, allocated monthly overhead, sales tax, a job log and a 12-month projection.

Is this a recommended rate for my city?

No. Example prices (for example a $60 base plus $20 per bedroom and $25 per bathroom on a standard clean, with a $120 minimum) are only there so you can see the formulas work. Replace them with your own figures. This is a planning aid, not pricing, tax, legal or accounting advice.
