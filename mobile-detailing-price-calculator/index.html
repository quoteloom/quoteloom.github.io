How to price a mobile detailing job

Price by package and vehicle size: an SUV, truck or van takes longer than a car, so each size gets its own price and time. Vehicles in rough shape take longer again, so a condition surcharge raises both the package price and the time. Extras such as pet hair, odor treatment, headlight restoration, engine bay, sealant and clay bar are priced as separate add-ons. If the total comes in under your minimum charge, use the minimum: a quick wash still costs you the drive and the setup.

Mobile work has a cost a shop doesn't: the drive. Count your drive time as paid time and your mileage at your real cost per mile, and charge a travel fee beyond a free radius if far-away jobs don't pay. Then check the bid: if the margin is below your target, raise the price to at least the “minimum price for target,” or walk away. The mobile detailing pricing guide walks through this same example step by step.

How it's calculated

  • Services = package price × (1 + condition surcharge) + each ticked add-on's price, or the minimum charge if that is higher (0 if nothing is selected).
  • Price per visit = services − membership discount + travel fee. Travel fee = one-way miles beyond the free radius × fee per mile (not discounted).
  • Worker-hours = package minutes × (1 + surcharge) + example add-on times (pet hair 30, odor 20, headlights 40, engine bay 30, sealant 45, clay bar 45 minutes) + water & power setup. Paid drive hours = one-way minutes × 2 / 60 × crew.
  • Labor = (worker-hours + paid drive hours) × labor cost per worker-hour. Vehicle cost = one-way miles × 2 × cost per mile.
  • Job cost = labor + chemicals & supplies + vehicle cost. Profit = price − job cost. Margin = profit / price. Minimum price = job cost / (1 − target margin).

Frequently asked questions

How do I price a mobile detailing job?

Start from a package price for the vehicle size (car, SUV, truck or van), add a surcharge for very dirty vehicles, then add extras like pet hair, odor, headlights, engine bay, sealant or clay bar. Add a travel fee beyond your free radius, use a minimum charge on small jobs, and check the price against your labor (including drive time), chemicals and vehicle costs.

What should I charge for a full detail?

There is no single market rate. This calculator ships with example prices ($160 car, $190 SUV, $210 truck, $230 van for a full detail, $40 to $80 add-ons, an $80 minimum and $1.50 per mile beyond 10 miles), the same example rates as the worked example in the Quoteloom mobile detailing guide. Replace them with your own.

Should I charge for travel?

Mobile work costs drive time and mileage on every job. This page counts both as costs, and lets you charge a travel fee per one-way mile beyond a free radius. Set the fee to 0 if you build travel into your package prices instead.

Does this include equipment wear and overhead?

No. The free calculator includes labor, drive time, chemicals and vehicle cost only. The spreadsheet also allocates equipment wear and monthly overhead to each job, prices water and power setup, and shows maintenance membership value, a printable quote, a job log and a 12-month projection.

Is this pricing advice?

No. It is a planning aid that uses only the numbers you enter, plus the example rates shown on the page. It is not a market survey or tax, legal, environmental or accounting advice.
